Methodology

What this calculates

A single 1–5 crustal thickness favourability score for a region of interest, by integrating the thickness, area, and time history of crust within that region. The calculation accounts for the fact that thickness varies in space and time — a small fraction of very thick crust for a brief period is not equivalent to widespread sustained moderately-thick crust.

The mathematics

Combined Favourability = Σ [ area_fraction(i) × time_fraction(i) × f(thickness_i) ]

For each block of the region — a portion of the assessment area at a particular thickness for a particular fraction of the lookback window — multiply its area fraction, its time fraction, and the favourability assigned to that thickness. Sum across all blocks. The result is a 0–1 number that maps to a 1–5 framework score.

The two-axis classification — tectonic setting × magmatic affinity

The favourability function f(h) and the importance value depend on which magmatic-hydrothermal system is being scored. Rather than asking the user to pick a deposit type, the framework asks two independent questions:

  • Tectonic Setting — what is the geodynamic environment? Controls the typical crustal thickness range for the system.
  • Magmatic Affinity — what is the magma chemistry? Controls how fertility is built and whether crustal thickness is the mechanism or just the context.

The combination determines the f(h) curve and the importance. Some combinations are geologically rare or inconsistent (e.g. tholeiitic in continental arc, alkalic in primitive island arc) and are flagged as "geologically unusual — reconsider classification."

Why this approach matters — Pueblo Viejo as the test case

Pueblo Viejo is a giant HS Au-Ag deposit (>40 Moz) hosted in primitive intra-oceanic island arc with crust ≤25 km. A simple "deposit-type-driven" calculator would either score it low and be embarrassed by the endowment, or score it high through some classification fudge.

The two-axis approach handles PV honestly: tectonic setting = primitive island arc; magmatic affinity = tholeiitic / low-K. This combination has importance 1 and an f(h) that peaks at 20–30 km. PV scores a high f(h) for its actual thickness, with importance 1 telling the assessor that thickness is not the prospectivity driver — fertility is source-derived and captured by the magmatic fertility score.

The lookback window

The lookback window (default 20 Ma) is the period immediately preceding the youngest mineralisation in the period of interest, looking backwards in time. Anchored to Loucks (2014) and Chiaradia & Caricchi (2017) timescales for sustained crustal thickness supporting deep magmatic systems.

20 Ma is the framework default. Adjust only for very young arcs younger than 20 Ma; Carlin-type or post-collisional systems where source preparation extends much further back; or Archean greenstone belts where geochronological resolution is coarser.

What "time fraction" means

Each block has a time fraction (0–1) representing the fraction of the lookback window over which that area was at that thickness. A block at "this thickness for the full window" has time fraction = 1.0; "for half the window" = 0.5.

Score mapping

≥0.90→ 5 (highly favourable)
0.70–0.90→ 4 (favourable)
0.50–0.70→ 3 (moderate)
0.30–0.50→ 2 (limited)
<0.30→ 1 (unfavourable)

Calibration: a region uniformly at thickness X for the full lookback window scores its base table value (e.g. uniform >50 km → 5; uniform 30–40 km → 3; uniform <20 km → 1).

Importance and the score

Each combination has an importance value (1, 2, or 3) reflecting how directly crustal thickness controls fertility:

  • 3 — direct mechanistic control. Score is a primary driver of prospectivity.
  • 2 — meaningful but secondary control. Score contributes alongside fertility.
  • 1 — geodynamic context indicator only. Score confirms tectonic setting only.

The calculator outputs an unweighted favourability score; importance is shown alongside the selected combination for context but is not folded into the score itself.

Coverage check

Σ(area × time) across all blocks should equal 1.0. If less, the calculator is treating the missing portion as unfavourable thickness — which may not be your intent.

Calibration status

Continental arc + calc-alkaline f(h) is calibrated against the framework base score table for porphyry Cu and stress-tested against worked examples (El Teniente, Chuquicamata, etc).

All other combinations have first-order f(h) tables consistent with the typical syn-mineral thickness ranges for known examples in each combination, but they are not yet calibrated against systematic data. Calibration is pending the deposit calibration dataset workflow — once syn-mineral crustal thickness is populated for a representative subset of high-endowment deposits, the f(h) curves can be derived empirically.

Until then, scores from non-continental-arc-calc-alkaline combinations should be treated as indicative rather than definitive.

Progressive thickening — a qualitative consideration

Where the crust thickened progressively into the syn-mineral window — i.e. measurably thicker at the moment of mineralisation than the lookback average — the integrated score should be considered a lower bound. A manual upgrade by one step may be warranted where there is good evidence for an active thickening trajectory hitting peak thickness at mineralisation, since this is a known signature of giant porphyry formation (e.g. flat-slab cessation in the Andes, post-collisional pulses in the Tethyan belt).
